The Night That Never Happened: Dec. 8, 1980

On a cold Monday evening in Manhattan, the world was told that John Winston Ono Lennon had been shot dead outside the Dakota building on West 72nd Street. The official story was sealed within hours. The shooter — a man with suspicious CIA connections and a copy of The Catcher in the Rye — was convicted in under two years. Case closed. Move along. Nothing to see.

Except: our sources say the body in the ambulance was not John Lennon. It was a remarkably well-prepared double — a contingency long rumoured to have existed since the FBI's COINTELPRO harassment campaign began targeting Lennon in 1971.

Nixon had tried to deport him. The FBI had 281 pages on him. The CIA flagged him as a “national security threat” for simply writing songs about peace. Is it so hard to believe that John Lennon — a man who knew they were watching — had an exit plan?

  • The Missing Hours: Between 10:49 PM (time of alleged shooting) and 11:07 PM (official hospital arrival), there is an 18-minute gap for a journey that takes 4 minutes. 18 MINUTES!!!
  • Forensic Audio: A Norwegian musicologist, Dr. K. Haanstad, has identified harmonic fingerprints consistent with Lennon's voiceprint in three post-1980 recordings attributed to other artists. The university declined to publish. He resigned. We are publishing.

John Lennon had the resources, the motivation, and — crucially — the imagination to disappear. He'd done it before, in his own way: the "Lost Weekend" of 1973–74 that lasted 18 months. He was, at heart, a man who believed reality was something you could opt out of.

We believe he opted out of this one.
